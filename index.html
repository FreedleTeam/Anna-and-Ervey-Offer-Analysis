The overall picture

What we're seeing

There is a lot to like about this property's underlying potential. Its location within the neighborhood is a positive, and the home appears to have a solid foundation for a future renovation. But it needs a comprehensive interior refresh and significant landscaping. The unfinished basement also limits usable square footage today.

Our perspective: We'd rather see you buy this at a price that leaves room to make it a home you love, rather than spend so much upfront that the renovation puts your total investment above the value of comparable finished homes.

Before moving forward

Our next steps

Price the renovation before committing

Get at least a preliminary contractor walkthrough for the kitchen, baths, flooring, landscaping and any layout changes you want.

Verify the expensive systems

Confirm roof, windows, furnace and water-heater age and condition; inspect the sewer, electrical, plumbing, drainage and foundation. Ask about options and costs for cooling.

Decide how much work you want to take on

Consider disruption, timing, permits and the realities of living through a renovation. Keep a meaningful contingency.

Negotiate around the total investment

Look at the purchase price and renovation together. A lower offer is only useful if the final project remains financially comfortable for you.

The bottom line

Potential is there. The price needs to reflect the project.

We like that this property has a good location within Village East and a layout with future potential. But our priority is helping you make a smart decision, not talking you into a project. At a price in the lower-to-middle $400,000s, the numbers begin to make more sense. Closer to full asking, we'd want much stronger evidence that the renovation will be less extensive than it currently appears.

Our guiding principle: Buy the house and the renovation together—not as two separate decisions.
